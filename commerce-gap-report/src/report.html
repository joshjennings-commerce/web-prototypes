The gap

Ready vs. really ready.

In six areas we compared how many ecommerce leaders are active or confident against how many have the foundations in place. The distance between the dots is the gap.

Finding 01 · AI adoption

Nearly everyone uses AI. Few let it near the money.

90%

of ecommerce leaders say their organization uses AI somewhere in its operation. Only 8% use it in checkout or payments.

Most AI use sits behind the scenes. The single most common job is writing product content such as titles, descriptions and attributes (52%). Three in four use some back-end AI; 60% use something customer-facing.

Use thins out where money changes hands. 16% use AI to set prices and 8% use it in checkout or payments. And only 11% are very or completely confident their product data meets the requirements of AI-powered targeting and bidding on retail media.

💡 Why it matters

A 90% adoption rate overstates how far most teams have gone. The low-risk, invisible work is done. The trust-sensitive work that agentic checkout will demand is still ahead, and it runs on product data few teams trust yet.

ReviewSM Q12, Q13. Report strand: both. Researcher confidence: high. The 11% (Q13) sits here as the data side of AI adoption; it could also lead the product data finding.

Finding 02 · Trust in AI

Trust drops the closer AI gets to a payment.

24%

believe customers have moderate or high trust in AI-driven personalized pricing, against 81% for AI search results.

We asked ecommerce leaders how much they think their customers trust five AI experiences. Search and recommendations score well. Chatbots split the room. Payments and pricing score lowest, and more than a third of respondents either don't use AI there or can't say.

This is what merchants believe about their customers, not what customers told us.

💡 Why it matters

Shoppers will let AI suggest things long before they let it pay for them. As agentic checkout rolls out, the brands that earn trust at the payment step will set the pace for everyone else.

ReviewSM Q14. Report strand: BigCommerce. Researcher confidence: medium-high. Keep the "merchants believe" framing in every reuse of this stat.

Finding 03 · AI search and agentic shopping

AI search is wide open. Few have a real plan for it.

18%

have a formal answer engine optimization (AEO/GEO) strategy with dedicated time or budget. 64% are doing something about it.

Agentic shopping is further behind. 9% have made real changes such as structured product data, agent-accessible checkout or APIs. 23% have taken no action.

Measurement lags too. 32% either don't track traffic from AI assistants like ChatGPT and Perplexity or can't measure any. And one in five can't say whether organic search traffic changed after AI Overviews launched.

💡 Why it matters

Awareness is close to universal and action is rare. That leaves an early-mover window open for brands that do the structural work now.

ReviewSM Q15–Q18. Feeds the AEO content strand. Check: the 32% includes "None that we can measure" (8%), which may mean zero traffic. "Don't track or can't measure" alone is 24%.

Finding 04 · Product data

Leaders trust their product data more than they check it.

28%

of ecommerce leaders are very or completely confident their product data is complete and accurate across channels.

Of that confident group, 38% have never audited their feeds or don't know if anyone has. Across all respondents, 41% have never run a formal feed audit and only 13% audit on a defined schedule.

Listing problems go unseen too. 37% either don't track how often channels reject or suppress their listings, or say they aren't aware of any rejections.

💡 Why it matters

Confidence that hasn't been tested is a guess. Disapproved and suppressed listings cost reach quietly, and you only find them if you look.

ReviewSM Q31, Q32, Q35. Report strand: Feedonomics headline. Researcher confidence: high. The 87% with no regular audit (doc, Part 2) matches the export.

Finding 05 · Feed management

Product feeds still run on manual effort.

39%

manage product feeds manually or mostly by hand. 13% are mostly or fully automated.

The hours add up. 23% of teams spend 11 or more hours a week on manual product-data tasks, more than a full working day.

Manual feeds follow company size. Half of small businesses (50%) and nearly half of mid-market brands (47%) run them by hand, compared with 15% of large enterprises.

💡 Why it matters

These teams already sell on five or more channels. Every channel multiplies the upkeep, and the hours go to maintenance instead of growth.

ReviewSM Q33, Q34. Report strand: Feedonomics. Researcher confidence: high on the what. The survey doesn't say why teams haven't automated; flagged as a follow-up question for next year.

Finding 06 · Channels

Multichannel is already the norm.

5.4

of eight major channels are active for the average brand. 82% sell on four or more.

Only 6% sell on two channels or fewer. Own websites are close to universal (97%), and most brands also run Meta, Google Shopping and a B2B or wholesale portal.

TikTok Shop is the least adopted channel (41%), but it has the steadiest growth among brands that use it: 67% grew revenue there and just 3% saw a decline, the lowest decline rate of any channel.

💡 Why it matters

Brands already have the channels. The pressure now is running all of them on the same manual tooling.

ReviewSM Q23. Report strand: BigCommerce. New cut: the per-channel growth view (and the TikTok Shop line) isn't in the findings doc yet. Needs a researcher check before it's used.

Finding 07 · Checkout

Nearly a quarter don't measure checkout abandonment.

23%

don't know or don't track their checkout abandonment rate.

The blind spot holds even among the most confident. 30% of ecommerce leaders who call their organization very or completely ready to grow don't track abandonment.

Among those who do measure it, most say it's flat or improving. The top factors limiting checkout conversion are poor mobile checkout (28%), complex or lengthy flows (25%) and no buy-now-pay-later option (20%).

💡 Why it matters

You can't fix what you don't measure. Abandonment is the most direct read on checkout health, and nearly one in four brands is planning growth without it.

ReviewSM Q24–Q26. Report strand: BigCommerce. Researcher call: lead with the blind spot, not the trend. The planned "abandonment is getting worse" angle didn't hold.

Finding 08 · Integrations

Broken integrations cost more than half of brands.

54%

had an integration failure hurt sales or operations in the past 12 months. 12% called it very or extremely significant.

An integration is any hand-off between systems: orders into shipping, inventory into marketplaces, checkout into payments. When one breaks, orders, stock or payments stop flowing correctly.

The same worry shapes platform decisions. Asked about changing platforms, 74% expect data migration to be hard, followed by integrations (59%) and budget or hidden costs (52%).

💡 Why it matters

Every hand-off is another place for the business to break. Fewer, native connections reduce the risk and the cost of the next move.

ReviewSM Q29, Q30. Report strand: BigCommerce. Researcher confidence: medium-high. Cite the survey's 54% separately from the 71% in the plan's desk research.

Finding 09 · Leadership and budget

Leadership is bought in. Budget hasn't followed.

40%

of ecommerce leaders say their leadership team understands the commercial impact of ecommerce infrastructure, but investment lags.

72% describe leadership as mostly or fully aligned, and only one respondent said it isn't aligned at all. But just 32% say ecommerce infrastructure is a stated priority with dedicated budget and ownership.

Planned spend leans toward media. Paid social (52%), AI personalization (49%) and Google Shopping (47%) top the list for the next 12 months. Product data and feed management comes fourth (31%).

10%

of the 40 brands planning more retail media spend are very or completely confident their product data meets AI targeting requirements. 45% of them have never audited their feeds.

💡 Why it matters

The case for investment is already made at the top. What's missing is budget for the plumbing, and the brands spending more on media are often the ones whose data can't yet support it.

ReviewSM Q11, Q20, Q13, Q35. Report strand: both. New cut: retail-media investors × data readiness was flagged as a to-do in the findings doc. Needs a researcher check.

    Methodology

    How we ran the study.

    We surveyed 141 senior ecommerce decision-makers at product-selling brands between September 15 and October 29, 2026. Respondents were recruited through User Interviews and the survey was administered in SurveyMonkey. 98.6% are the primary decision-maker or directly involved in decisions about their ecommerce platform, tools or vendors.

    • Percentages are shares of all 141 respondents unless a smaller group is named. Multi-select questions can total more than 100%.
    • Comparisons between groups are descriptive and not formal significance tests. Treat groups under 40 respondents as directional.
    • The customer-trust question asks ecommerce leaders what they believe their customers feel. It isn't a customer survey.
    • Regional results are directional, especially for Asia-Pacific (n=25). Latin America isn't represented.
    • Quality checks flagged four responses (3%). Excluding them doesn't change any finding, so all 141 are included.
